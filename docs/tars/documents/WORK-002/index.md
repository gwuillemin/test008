# New Zealand family holiday itinerary

**Travel dates:** 13 September–2 October (year to be confirmed)  
**Travellers:** Family with children aged 9 and 11  
**Route:** Wellington → Picton → Nelson → Kaikōura → Christchurch → Lake Tekapo → Wānaka → Queenstown

## Route at a glance

This itinerary uses Wellington's public transport rather than keeping a car in the city, crosses Cook Strait as foot passengers, and picks up a one-way South Island hire car in Picton. It reaches Queenstown on **22 September** and then uses one base for the remainder of the trip. Approximate driving times below are wheels-turning times; allow extra time for food, toilets, viewpoints, roadworks, and spring weather.

| Date | Overnight | Main travel | Approximate driving |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 13–14 Sep | Wellington (2 nights) | Airport transfer; city on foot/public transport | None |
| 15 Sep | Picton | Cook Strait ferry | None |
| 16 Sep | Nelson | Picton–Nelson | 110 km / 2 hr |
| 17 Sep | Kaikōura | Nelson–Kaikōura | 250 km / 3 hr 45 min |
| 18–19 Sep | Christchurch (2 nights) | Kaikōura–Christchurch | 180 km / 2 hr 30 min |
| 20 Sep | Lake Tekapo | Christchurch–Tekapo | 230 km / 3 hr |
| 21 Sep | Wānaka | Tekapo–Wānaka | 200 km / 2 hr 45 min |
| 22 Sep–2 Oct | Queenstown (10 nights before departure) | Wānaka–Queenstown, then local outings | 70 km / 1 hr 15 min |

The longest road day is 17 September. It is bracketed by a short day and a moderate day, followed by a no-drive day. The later three-day progression consists of moderate, short-to-moderate, and short drives—not consecutive long hauls.

## Day-by-day plan

### 13 September — Arrive in Wellington

Transfer from the airport to a central hotel and keep the day deliberately light. If energy and weather allow, walk the waterfront and Oriental Bay; otherwise settle in and eat nearby. No hire car is needed in compact central Wellington.

**Sleep:** Wellington  
**Flex:** Treat everything except arrival and check-in as optional.

### 14 September — Wellington discovery day

Spend the morning at **Te Papa**, whose interactive natural-history and Māori cultural exhibits suit both ages. After lunch, choose the Wellington Cable Car and Botanic Garden walk downhill, or Zealandia's family-friendly sanctuary trails and native birds. Leave time to buy ferry snacks and repack a small day bag.

**Sleep:** Wellington  
**Driving:** None; use walking, bus, or taxi/rideshare.

### 15 September — Cook Strait to Picton

Take a morning or midday **Wellington–Picton ferry as foot passengers**. The crossing is normally about 3.5 hours and the Marlborough Sounds approach is a scenic experience in itself. Collect the South Island hire car near the Picton terminal only if the desk remains open after the booked sailing; otherwise collect it the next morning. Stretch legs on the Picton waterfront and the short Bob's Bay/Essons Valley options as conditions and daylight permit.

**Sleep:** Picton  
**Driving:** None or local only.  
**Important:** Ferry schedules can change and Cook Strait sailings can be delayed by weather. Do not book a time-critical activity that afternoon.

### 16 September — Picton to Nelson

Collect the car if not already done, fit/check child restraints if required by the operator, and drive Queen Charlotte Drive toward Nelson. Use Havelock for a break and lunch. If the winding scenic road is uncomfortable, use the operator-recommended main-road route instead. In Nelson, enjoy the waterfront, Founders Heritage Park, or an easy beach stop at Tāhunanui.

**Sleep:** Nelson  
**Driving:** About 110 km / 2 hours, plus stops.

### 17 September — Nelson to Kaikōura

Make an early, unhurried start for the trip via Blenheim to Kaikōura. Break in Blenheim and again at a safe coastal viewpoint; pack lunch or identify opening hours in advance. On arrival, take the easy **Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway** section to see coastal scenery and, from a respectful distance, seals. Keep at least 20 metres from seals and never get between one and the sea.

**Sleep:** Kaikōura  
**Driving:** About 250 km / 3 hours 45 minutes before breaks; this is the longest driving day.

### 18 September — Kaikōura wildlife, then Christchurch

Reserve the morning for a weather-dependent whale-watching cruise or family-suitable wildlife experience. Acknowledge seasickness risk and minimum-age/operator rules; the peninsula walk is the free fallback. Drive south after lunch, stopping at a beach or Amberley for a break, and arrive in Christchurch before dinner.

**Sleep:** Christchurch  
**Driving:** About 180 km / 2 hours 30 minutes, plus stops.

### 19 September — Christchurch rest and play

A no-drive recovery day. Combine the **Antarctic Centre** (interactive and weatherproof) with only one gentle city activity: Riverside Market, Margaret Mahy Playground, the Botanic Gardens, or the tram. Keep the afternoon open for laundry, downtime, or bad-weather reshuffling.

**Sleep:** Christchurch  
**Driving:** None apart from optional local travel.

### 20 September — Christchurch to Lake Tekapo

Drive across the Canterbury Plains, breaking around Geraldine for food and a playground. Continue to Lake Tekapo for lakeshore views and the Church of the Good Shepherd exterior. After dinner, stargaze independently if skies are clear or join a pre-booked family-compatible astronomy session. September nights can be very cold.

**Sleep:** Lake Tekapo  
**Driving:** About 230 km / 3 hours, plus stops.

### 21 September — Lake Tekapo to Wānaka

Follow the scenic route past Lake Pūkaki, with a viewpoint stop if Aoraki/Mount Cook is visible, then cross Lindis Pass. Break in Twizel or Ōmarama and do not add the Mount Cook road unless the family is happy with a substantially longer day. In Wānaka, walk the lakefront and find #ThatWānakaTree; **Puzzling World** is a good indoor or high-energy option if arrival time permits.

**Sleep:** Wānaka  
**Driving:** About 200 km / 2 hours 45 minutes, plus stops.

### 22 September — Wānaka to Queenstown

Enjoy a slow morning: Puzzling World, a lakeside walk, or downtime. Drive to Queenstown using the **Cromwell/SH6 route**, the simpler default in cold or uncertain weather. Crown Range Road is shorter but higher and should be used only if road and weather conditions are suitable and the driver is comfortable. Settle into the Queenstown base and walk the lakefront.

**Sleep:** Queenstown  
**Driving:** About 70 km / 1 hour 15 minutes via the Crown Range, or roughly 115 km / 1 hour 40 minutes via Cromwell; allow more in winter conditions.  
**Milestone:** Arrive in Queenstown on 22 September.

### 23 September — Queenstown orientation

Keep the first full day local. Ride the Skyline gondola and, if operating and age/height rules are met, try the luge; alternatively explore Queenstown Gardens and play disc golf. Leave a free afternoon for rest, swimming at the accommodation, or the lakefront.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 24 September — Arrowtown and easy trails

Visit historic Arrowtown, explore the riverside trail, and choose a child-friendly museum or gold-panning activity. Add a short stop in the Gibbston Valley only if it offers a genuinely family-oriented activity; this is not planned as an adult tasting day. Return early for a quiet evening.

**Sleep:** Queenstown  
**Driving:** Roughly 40–70 km total depending on stops.

### 25 September — Flexible weather/rest day

Hold this day unscheduled. Use it for laundry, a relaxed café morning, Queenstown Gardens, an indoor climbing/swimming option, or to move an outdoor booking from a poor-weather day. This buffer is intentional rather than a missed sightseeing day.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 26 September — Milford Sound without a family driving marathon

Take a pre-booked **coach-and-cruise day trip** from Queenstown, subject to road and weather conditions. It is a long day (often around 12–13 hours), but a coach avoids approximately eight hours of self-driving and lets everyone enjoy the scenery. Bring layers, rain protection, food, and entertainment for the coach. If that duration is unsuitable, replace it with a shorter Doubtful Sound trip only after checking its duration/logistics, or use this as a local free day.

**Sleep:** Queenstown  
**Driving:** None by the family.

### 27 September — Recovery day

Sleep in after Milford. Choose a short lakeside outing, the Kiwi Park (check feeding/presentation times), or a quiet indoor activity. Avoid scheduling another early start or lengthy drive.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 28 September — Glenorchy scenery

Take a day trip along the shore of Lake Wakatipu to Glenorchy, stopping only at safe pull-offs. Choose a short, conditions-appropriate walk such as the Glenorchy Lagoon walkway, have lunch, and return before dark. If weather is poor, defer this outing to 30 September and use a local indoor day instead.

**Sleep:** Queenstown  
**Driving:** About 90 km / 1 hour 45 minutes return, before stops.

### 29 September — Family choice day

Choose **one** major activity to avoid overloading the day: a lake cruise (with or without a farm visit), a gentle guided cycle ride, or a family-suitable jet-boat experience. Confirm age, height, health, clothing, and weather restrictions before booking. Keep the remainder of the day free.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 30 September — Outdoor buffer

Use this as the second weather buffer. Options include the deferred Glenorchy trip, an easy section of the Queenstown Trail, or a short walk at Bob's Cove if track and road conditions are good. Avoid committing to a remote or alpine hike in uncertain spring conditions.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 1 October — Final relaxed day

Enjoy a favourite repeat activity, souvenir shopping, and a final lakefront walk. Refuel the car, confirm the airport return location and opening hours, pack, and check the flight status. Keep the evening early and avoid any weather-dependent excursion that could jeopardise departure.

**Sleep:** Queenstown

### 2 October — Depart Queenstown

Check out, return the hire car at Queenstown Airport with the agreed fuel level, and allow extra time for inspection, luggage, and security. Departure timing and any morning activity depend entirely on the confirmed flight; none is assumed.

## Transport and car-hire plan

1. **Wellington:** Use an airport transfer and public transport. This avoids city parking and prevents paying for a car during the ferry crossing.
2. **Cook Strait:** Book Wellington–Picton as foot passengers and reserve seats together. Confirm baggage allowances, check-in cut-off, terminal transfers, and sailing status directly with the ferry operator.
3. **South Island car:** Book a one-way rental **Picton pickup on 15 or 16 September, Queenstown Airport return on 2 October**. Confirm the one-way fee, unlimited kilometres, insurance/excess, additional drivers, roadside support, late-ferry pickup procedure, and whether snow chains are supplied or required.
4. **Do not assume a rental can cross on the ferry.** Many firms split North and South Island hires or restrict vehicles on Cook Strait services. The foot-passenger/separate-car plan avoids that issue.
5. **Contingency:** If the ferry is cancelled, ask the operator about rebooking, then investigate a Wellington–Blenheim flight and transfer to Picton without assuming availability. Notify both Picton accommodation and the rental desk immediately. Retain a flexible first South Island booking where possible.
6. **Road safety:** Check NZTA/Waka Kotahi journey information and MetService each travel morning. September can bring frost, snow, high winds, slips, and road closures. Drive on the left, build in daylight, never stop in live traffic for photos, and change the route rather than press on in unsafe conditions.

## Assumptions

- The **travel year is not yet specified**. Dates are therefore shown without weekdays; school holidays, public holidays, operating seasons, and exact schedules must be checked for the chosen year.
- Arrival is at Wellington Airport on 13 September and departure is from Queenstown Airport on 2 October; flight times are unknown.
- All travellers can enter New Zealand and meet airline, ferry, rental, and activity requirements.
- A parent/guardian is licensed and comfortable driving on the left. Drive times are approximate and exclude breaks.
- The family prefers comfortable, moderately paced sightseeing over strenuous alpine hiking. No accessibility, dietary, medical, or mobility requirements have been supplied.
- Accommodation is envisaged as family rooms or apartments, ideally with kitchen and laundry access, but no lodging or budget level has been selected.
- Activities named here are suggestions, not confirmed reservations. Operators, tracks, and seasonal attractions may be unavailable.

## Details to confirm before booking

- **Dates and flights:** travel year, Wellington arrival time, Queenstown departure time, baggage, airport transfers, and whether jet lag requires more rest.
- **Budget:** total and daily targets; accommodation standard; appetite for paid wildlife, gondola, cruise, and adventure activities.
- **Accommodation:** availability and cancellation terms for every overnight, parking in Picton onward, kitchen/laundry needs, and room/bed configuration.
- **Ferry:** sailing time, terminal location and transfer, check-in deadline, luggage rules, flexible/cancellation terms, and disruption policy.
- **Rental car:** Picton depot hours matched to ferry arrival, Queenstown Airport return, vehicle/luggage capacity, automatic transmission, insurance/excess, one-way fee, snow-chain policy, breakdown cover, and approved drivers. Confirm current New Zealand child-restraint law and rental-company requirements for each child based on age and height.
- **Activities:** operating calendars, family age/height/health rules, weather cancellation/refund terms, seasickness considerations, equipment/clothing, and advance-booking needs—especially Kaikōura wildlife, astronomy, Skyline/luge, Milford coach-cruise, and the chosen family activity on 29 September.
- **Road conditions:** check Cook Strait weather, SH1, Lindis Pass, the Queenstown approaches, and the Milford Road close to travel. Keep the Christchurch, Queenstown, and outdoor buffer days movable.
- **Family preferences:** tolerance for the long coach day to Milford, desired walking distance, wildlife priorities, dietary needs, and whether any traveller needs accessibility support.

## Practical booking order

After confirming the year and flights, secure the Cook Strait crossing and South Island one-way car together so their times align. Then book the limited one-night stops (Picton, Nelson, Kaikōura, Tekapo, and Wānaka), followed by Christchurch and the longer Queenstown stay. Reserve only the highest-priority weather-sensitive activities with flexible terms, preserving the buffer days rather than filling every gap.
